What Systeme.io's free plan actually includes: every published limit, checked

Published: November 9, 2026 · Last updated: November 9, 2026 · Facts checked: November 9, 2026

This is a check of Systeme.io's published pricing page and its FAQ, not a hands-on review. Figures are as published by the vendor and checked on the date above; the source is listed at the end. This article contains no affiliate links at the time of publication.

Systeme.io's free plan is usually summarized as "2,000 contacts, free forever." Both halves of that sentence are on the official pricing page — the plan never expires and needs no credit card — but the plan's usefulness is decided by the smaller limits underneath the headline, and by what the vendor says happens when you reach one. Here is the full published list.

Read as a set, the free plan is one complete small business, once: one funnel set (up to 3 funnels), one course with up to 500 students, one blog on one custom domain, one affiliate program of your own, and email to 2,000 contacts with no send cap. The binding constraints for most people will not be the contact ceiling — they will be the single automation rule and the single campaign. A welcome sequence plus a re-engagement rule is already two automations; the second one does not fit.

What happens when you pass a limit

Systeme.io publishes this in its pricing FAQ, and it is more specific than most vendors get. When an account goes over a plan limit, the vendor sends a warning email each day for 3 days and shows an alert on the dashboard; the items over the limit are highlighted in the plan settings. If nothing changes by day 4:

  • On the free plan, the account is restricted: opt-in forms and payments stop working, new contacts and students cannot be added, emails and new automations pause, and students cannot access courses.
  • On a paid plan, the subscription is automatically upgraded to the next plan instead of being restricted.
  • Nothing is deleted, including student progress, and the restriction lifts once you upgrade or return within your limits.

That last point is the one to plan around: on the free plan, crossing a limit is not a soft nudge, it is a stop on lead capture and payments after a 3-day grace period. If a launch is likely to push you past 2,000 contacts or a second automation, the upgrade decision belongs before the launch, not after the warning emails start.

The published upgrade is a capacity purchase, not a feature unlock: the same toolset with the ceilings moved. The next steps on the same page are Webinar ($47/month; 10,000 contacts, 50 funnels, 20 courses, evergreen webinars) and Unlimited ($97/month; unlimited contacts, funnels, courses and automation rules). Two further terms from the same FAQ are worth knowing before you rely on a paid plan as a trial: you can cancel at any time, and a cancelled account keeps its plan until the next billing date and then reverts to the free plan — so an experiment on Startup does not strand your funnels if you step back down, as long as you are within the free limits again.

The verdict, on the published terms

The free plan is enough if…you run one offer: one funnel, one course, one automation (a single welcome or delivery rule), under 2,000 contacts and 500 students. Within those walls, nothing in the published terms forces a payment — sends are unlimited and the plan does not expire.
The free plan is not enough if…you need a second automation rule, a second course, or a list above 2,000 contacts — or if a campaign might push you over a limit mid-launch, given the documented 3-day warning and restriction sequence. In those cases Startup at $17/month is priced as the release valve, and the comparison that matters is against the cost of the separate tools it replaces, not against $0.
