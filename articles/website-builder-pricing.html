Website Builder Pricing: Webflow vs Framer — Free Plans, First Paid Steps and Site Limits, Checked Against Official Pricing

Published: November 14, 2026 · Last updated: November 14, 2026 · Facts checked: November 14, 2026

This is a check of published pricing and help pages, not a hands-on review: we did not build production sites on these platforms for this article. Every figure below comes from the vendor's own pages, listed at the end, and was checked on the date above. Disclosure: Stack Tools Lab may earn a commission through affiliate links on this site. This article contains no affiliate links at the time of publication — the relevant programs are still under approval — and any links added later will be affiliate links.

Both builders let you start for free, but the free plans ration different things. Webflow's free Starter Site plan, as published on its pricing page, caps a site at 2 static pages, 1 GB of bandwidth and 50 form submissions, on a Webflow.io domain with a limited CMS. Framer's pricing FAQ describes a free plan with 10 CMS collections and 1,000 pages — and states, in the same answer, that connecting a custom domain requires upgrading to a paid plan. Read side by side, Webflow's free tier is a small test site, while Framer's free tier is a larger project that cannot yet go live on your own domain. The binding constraint is different before price enters the question.

The page counts are not directly comparable, and that is the point worth keeping. Webflow publishes its free cap as static pages on a hosted site plan (2), next to bandwidth and form submissions. Framer publishes its free allowance as project scope — CMS collections and pages — in a FAQ answer about exploring the tool and creating templates, and reserves the custom domain for paid plans. A two-page Webflow test and a 1,000-page Framer project are both "free", but only one of them is shaped like a published site, and neither is publishable on a custom domain for free on the terms quoted here: Webflow's Starter is on a Webflow.io domain, and Framer states the domain step itself is paid.

One cell in that table is deliberately not a number, following the same rule as our earlier pricing checks. Framer's pricing page renders its paid plan cards separately from the FAQ text; the FAQ fixes the structure — a custom domain requires a paid plan, editors are billed per seat, paid plans include monthly credits shared across the workspace — and those structural facts are what we quote. The monthly-billing price for Webflow's Basic Site plan is likewise not quoted: Webflow's pricing page, in the text checked here, publishes Basic at $15 per month billed yearly, and does not state a monthly-billing Site plan price in that same checkable text. Bandwidth add-on prices, which the page does publish for both billing cycles, are a different line item and are not a substitute for the plan price.

Webflow also separates Site plans from Workspace plans, and the pricing page is explicit that the two are different purchases: a Site plan is per site, while the free Workspace plan is where sites are staged, with its own staging-site limits (2 staging sites on the free Workspace, per the FAQ). Upgrading the place where you build and upgrading the site you publish are separate steps, and a budget that counts only one of them is incomplete in the same way a per-user SaaS quote that ignores seat minimums is incomplete.

What happens when you cross a limit

The two vendors publish different answers. Framer's FAQ states that at certain limits you will be asked to upgrade inside Framer, while for other limits, such as bandwidth, Framer allows you to exceed the limit for one month and notifies you by email so you can upgrade. Framer also states that editors are billed per seat, can be added to a workspace or project at any time, and are charged within 24 hours after a notification. Webflow's FAQ, in the text checked here, addresses cancellation of Site and Workspace plans — you can cancel at any time, but you still pay the remainder of the term for the plan you signed up for — and describes surge protection on paid plans, with additional bandwidth capacity available as an add-on at the published per-block prices.

Which one, by situation

You want to test a builder on a real, small site for freeWebflow's Starter, on the published numbers: it is a hosted site plan, but the scope is a test — 2 static pages, 1 GB of bandwidth and 50 form submissions on a Webflow.io domain. Treat anything larger as a paid-plan decision from the start.
You want to design and structure a larger project before payingFramer's free plan, on the published FAQ terms: 10 CMS collections and 1,000 pages of project scope, with collaboration for up to three editors in a workspace without a subscription. The step you cannot skip is the custom domain: Framer states that connecting one requires a paid plan.
You will pay, and the site is a simple site without a CMSWebflow Basic is the one fully priced first step in this comparison: $15 per month billed yearly, per site, adding a custom domain, 300 static pages, 10 GB of bandwidth and unlimited form submissions. If the site needs a CMS, the relevant Webflow comparison is Premium at $25 per month billed yearly, not Basic.
None of the free math fits if…your plan assumes a free custom domain on either builder, a Framer paid price quoted from a third-party roundup rather than Framer's own page, or a Webflow budget that counts the Site plan but not the separate Workspace plan where the site is staged.
