Project Management SaaS: Free vs Paid Entry Tiers, Checked Against Official Pricing

Published: November 13, 2026 · Last updated: November 13, 2026 · Facts checked: November 13, 2026

This is a check of published pricing and help pages, not a hands-on review: we did not run projects on these platforms for this article. Every figure below comes from the vendor's own pages, listed at the end, and was checked on the date above. Disclosure: Stack Tools Lab may earn a commission through affiliate links on this site. This article contains no affiliate links at the time of publication — the relevant programs are still under approval — and any links added later will be affiliate links.

The four first paid steps look like a tight race: $9 (ClickUp), $10.99 (Asana), $9 (monday.com) per user per month on annual billing, with Notion charging per member on the same pattern. Read with the free tiers attached, the race changes shape — because each free tier caps a different resource. Asana caps people (two users). monday.com caps seats and boards (two seats, three boards). ClickUp caps storage (60 MB) while leaving members unlimited. Notion caps content, and only once a second member joins. Which cap you hit first decides which tool is actually free for you, and it is decided before price enters the question.

The practical difference: a three-person team is over the free limit at Asana and monday.com on day one, regardless of how little work it tracks. The same team fits inside ClickUp's free tier until its attachments pass 60 MB, and inside Notion's free tier until the shared block allowance runs out — Notion's pricing FAQ states that at the limit you can still read, edit and organize existing blocks, but cannot add new ones. None of the four caps tasks: ClickUp, Asana and monday.com (on paid tiers) all publish unlimited tasks or items, so the binding constraint is never the work itself — it is the people, the files or the content volume around it.

The first paid step buys different things

VendorFirst paid planWhat the step actually buys
ClickUpCoreHeadroom and features together: unlimited storage, spaces, forms and Gantt charts, custom fields and task types, goals, and 1,000 automations per month
AsanaStarterThe team itself: no user seat limits, timeline and Gantt views, reporting dashboards, unlimited automations, forms and custom fields
monday.comBasicCapacity, not workflow: unlimited boards and items, unlimited free viewers, storage 500 MB → 5 GB. Automations and integrations are not included — they start on Standard (250 actions of each per month, per monday's pricing guide)

The monday.com row is the one most often misread. Basic is the cheapest first step in this comparison at $9 per seat, but it is a shared-board tier: a team that upgrades to Basic specifically to automate status updates has bought the wrong tier, because the automation allowance (250 actions a month) sits one step up on Standard, published at $12 per seat per month billed annually in monday's pricing guide. Asana runs the opposite structure: Starter, its first step, already includes unlimited automations, so the $10.99 figure and monday's $9 figure are not prices for the same thing.

The minimum real bill is not the headline price

Three of the four vendors attach a scope rule to the per-user price, and the rule — not the rate — sets the smallest possible invoice. monday.com's pricing FAQ states that plans start from 3 users: at the published Basic rate, the smallest paid position is 3 seats × $9 = $27 per month on annual billing, even for a two-person team leaving the free tier. ClickUp's FAQ states that upgrading means upgrading the entire Workspace, so a workspace with 12 members (including people who only occasionally comment) bills 12 Core seats from day one. Notion's billing help states that each paid workspace is billed separately by its own member count, so the same person in two paid workspaces is two paid seats. Asana is the exception in this set: Starter has no seat minimum beyond the users you actually add, and its unlimited free guests are explicitly outside the license count.

What happens when you cross a free limit

Each vendor publishes a different answer, and it is worth reading before you need it. ClickUp's FAQ describes "uses" as cumulative through the life of the Workspace: when a use limit is reached you lose no data, but you cannot create or edit new items with that feature until you upgrade, and ClickUp describes a grace period at usage limits. Notion's FAQ is the softest landing of the four: existing content stays fully editable and only adding blocks stops. Asana and monday.com publish no gradual consequence, because their free caps are structural — a third user or seat simply cannot be added on the free plan, so the limit announces itself at the moment of invitation rather than after the fact.

Which one, by situation

You are one or two people tracking your own workAsana's Personal plan, on the published numbers: unlimited tasks, projects and storage with list, board and calendar views, and the 2-user cap does not bind. monday.com's free plan fits the same size of team but caps you at 3 boards and 500 MB of storage.
Your team is bigger than two, and the budget is $0 for nowClickUp's Free Forever is the only free tier here with unlimited members: the constraint to watch is the 60 MB storage ceiling and the cumulative "uses" on paid features. Notion's free plan is the alternative if your work is documents and databases rather than task boards — unlimited while you work solo, capped on blocks once the workspace becomes a team.
You will pay, and automations are the reasonCompare Asana Starter ($10.99 per user per month billed annually, unlimited automations included) against monday.com Standard ($12 per seat billed annually, 250 automation and 250 integration actions per month) — not against monday Basic, which includes none. ClickUp Core includes 1,000 automations per month at $9, but the bill covers every member of the Workspace.
None of the four fits if…your headcount math ignores the scope rules: a monday.com quote that assumes one seat (the minimum is three), a ClickUp quote that counts only project members (the whole Workspace is billed), or a Notion quote that assumes one subscription covers several workspaces (each is billed separately). Run your actual roster against each billing scope before the per-user rate means anything.
